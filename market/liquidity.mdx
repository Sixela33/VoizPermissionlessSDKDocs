You are putting up inventory so people can trade “Will ETH be above $5,000 at year end?” You are not buying Yes or No.

You deposit collateral (USDC on Base and Polygon) into that question’s market maker. The contract holds a mix of Yes, No, and Invalid. Traders buy and sell through that inventory. Protocol, creator, and referrer fees leave the trade as cash. The liquidity-provider cut (default 0.8%) stays in the pool as extra complete sets.

Your profit and loss is that inventory marked at the market maker’s prices (or 0 or 1 after the result) versus what you put in. It is not deposit times odds times win or lose. Worked numbers (100 collateral, 70/30, buy 40): [LP calculator](/market/lp-calculator).

Not financial advice. Size only what you can lose.

## What you actually mint

You pass one collateral amount. The market maker pulls it, splits a complete set, keeps a slice of each answer as inventory, and mints ERC-20 pool shares to you. Those shares are a claim on the whole inventory — not an NFT and not a perpetual.

In code, first liquidity is `market.seed`. Later liquidity is `market.addLiquidity`. Both call `addFunding`.

```
collateral C
└── addFunding(C, hint)
    ├── market maker keeps answer tokens (inventory)
    ├── leftover tokens of lighter answers return to you
    └── you receive ERC-20 pool shares
```

| | Seed (`market.seed`) | Later LP (`market.addLiquidity`) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contract | `addFunding` | `addFunding` |
| Hint | Named-answer odds, Invalid = 0 | Empty — join current proportions |
| Shares minted | `C` | `C * supply / max(reserves)` |

Invalid is part of the complete set and sits in the market maker. It is not tradable and always prices at 0.

On a skewed pool, later liquidity binds on the heaviest reserve. Lighter answers send leftover tokens back to you.

## What you earn

| Source | Who gets it |
| ------ | ----------- |
| Protocol 1% + creator 0.5% + referrer 0.2% (defaults) | Treasury / creator / referrer (cash) |
| LP 0.8% (default) | Pool inventory (complete sets) |
| Mark vs cost | LP share lot |

Cost on add is `max(amountsAdded)` (`addedFunds`). Indexer remove proceeds are the **named** basket at 1e18 prices, or 0/1 if resolved. Send-back tokens are a **share position**, not this LP lot.

```
P&L  ≈  realized on burns  +  mark(remaining shares)  −  remaining cost
mark = shares / supply × Σ_named (reserve_i × priceCt_i)
```

A market that resolves quickly can show almost no trading and a large inventory term. The halt at resolve protects remaining inventory from 0/1 arb.

## Risks

### Trading stops when the market resolves — otherwise LPs get arbitraged

After payouts are set, every named share is worth **exactly `1` or `0` collateral**. If swaps kept running:

- **Winner still priced below 1** — buy it from the market maker, redeem at 1.
- **Loser still priced above 0** — sell worthless shares into the market maker, take collateral out.

`buy` / `sell` therefore revert `Resolved` once Conditional Tokens has a payout denominator. Fee income from traders stops with them.

Fee income for the protocol only exists **between the first deposit and resolve**. A market that resolves quickly can pay almost no volume relative to the inventory you posted — but leaving the market maker open would be worse: the inventory itself would be picked off.

### Inventory while the market is open

Across-answer liquidity is how you market-make the whole question without picking Yes or No. It is **not delta-neutral**. As implied probability moves, you hold more of the answer traders are selling. You are the other side of every trade.

### Even join vs live odds

Later `addLiquidity` joins **current** reserve proportions. It does not re-apply your original opening mix.

### Idle Invalid

The complete-set mint always prints Invalid. It never trades. Unwinding a full set later means you need the named answers back (from `removeFunding`, from trading, or from leftover send-backs).

### Operational

Approvals, factory version, and market-maker clone all have to match. Tiny deposits revert (`mintAmount == 0` or dust). Contracts, oracles, and resolution can fail.

This is not financial advice. Size only what you can lose.

## How to exit

1. `client.user(owner).lpPositions({ market })` — find the pool-share lot.
2. Open: `market.removeFunding({ shares, owner })` — basket of every slot, then `merge` complete sets if you want collateral.
3. Resolved: `market.redeemLp({ shares, owner })` — burn shares, redeem the basket to collateral (same redeem fee).
4. Send-back / leftover named tokens: `market.redeem` as share positions.

`redeemLp` only works after resolve. While open there is no 1:1 payout on a single answer.

## In the SDK

```ts
await market.seed({ seedWei, oddsBps, owner })
await market.addLiquidity({ credits, owner })
```
